# MOVIE-90M-001: The River Keeps the Names

**Production level:** 90+ minute movie  
**Target runtime:** at least 90 minutes, target about 100 minutes  
**Purpose:** Example and end-to-end test prompt for Story-Film Skills.

## Prompt

```text
Use Story-Film Skills to create a complete original feature film with a final runtime of at least 90 minutes. Target about 100 minutes.

Working title: The River Keeps the Names

Genre: Family drama and historical mystery.

Premise: After the death of a respected local boat builder, his estranged adult children return to settle his workshop and sell the property. They discover a hand-drawn river map covered with names, dates, and marks that do not match official records. Their search reveals that their father spent decades documenting people, homes, cemeteries, and communities displaced by floods, development, and changing river boundaries. A planned demolition will erase the last physical evidence of one forgotten settlement. The siblings disagree over whether preserving that history is worth risking the sale that each of them needs for different reasons.

Build a feature-length dramatic structure with strong character arcs, reversals, quiet scenes, discoveries, setbacks, and a resolved central choice. Avoid turning the story into a simple good-versus-evil argument. Give opposing characters reasonable motives.

Use sequence-level production from the start. Track family history, map annotations, letters, dates, ownership, weather, river levels, workshop objects, locations, character knowledge, relationships, and physical changes over time. Historical claims shown as real-world facts must use evidence-backed research. Fictional local history must be clearly treated as fiction.

Take the project through full creative discovery, research where needed, feature screenplay, `SEQ-###` planning, context shards, long-range continuity, production health, preproduction, generation budgeting, resource-safe ComfyUI batches, selective retry, take approval, sequence editorial, full editorial reconciliation, audio/video finishing, delivery QC, final feature master, completeness audit, trailer, social campaign, and release package.

Use original characters, dialogue, music direction, artwork, and visual designs. Do not imitate an existing film or living artist.

Testing goal: prove that Story-Film Skills can preserve emotional, historical, prop, location, and editorial state across a grounded 90+ minute feature and can recover cleanly across many sessions and generation batches.
```

## Test notes

Use the prompt without changes for a full production test. For a faster planning test, use the test modes in [Examples and test prompts](README.md).
