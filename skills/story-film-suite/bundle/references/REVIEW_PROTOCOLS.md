# Creative Review Protocols

Review and revision are different jobs. Diagnose first. Rewrite only after the user or workflow accepts the diagnosis.

## Story review levels

Use the highest useful level before lower ones.

1. **Editorial**: What kind of revision does the work need, and in what order?
2. **Developmental**: premise delivery, causality, structure, pacing, scene necessity, character arcs, promises, and payoffs.
3. **Scene**: immediate objective, resistance, turn, aftermath, point of view, information flow, and transition into the next scene.
4. **Line**: voice, rhythm, clarity, image selection, paragraph movement, dialogue, and unnecessary explanation.
5. **Copy**: grammar, usage, punctuation, terminology, capitalization, and local consistency.
6. **Proof**: final surface mistakes after content and wording are locked.

Do not spend time proofing paragraphs that may be deleted by a developmental pass.

## Reader or audience simulation

Simulation answers a different question from craft critique: what was the experience of receiving the work?

Always define or state:

- medium: prose, screenplay read, storyboard/animatic, or rough cut
- audience persona or target segment
- genre familiarity
- tolerance for ambiguity when relevant
- knowledge boundary: exactly what this audience member knows before the scope begins

Track only signals the material supports:

- attention and flow
- curiosity and prediction
- emotional alignment or distance
- character legibility
- spatial or action clarity for visual work
- expectation, surprise, and payoff response
- moments where the audience becomes aware of the construction instead of the story

Do not present one simulated persona as a universal audience verdict.

## Character simulation

A character simulation is a pressure test, not canon generation.

The simulation receives:

- stable character ID
- story moment or scene ID
- current goal
- relationship context
- emotional pressure
- available knowledge
- forbidden future knowledge

Stay inside the character's information boundary. Let them evade, misunderstand, joke, stall, contradict themselves, or refuse when that follows from established behavior. Do not make them explain the story's theme or hidden plot merely because the model knows it.

After a simulation, observations may inform a proposed revision, but no improvised fact enters canon without an explicit upstream edit.

## Modification verification

When a revision, adaptation, condensation, or expansion changes existing material:

1. state the intended change
2. state the facts and effects that must survive
3. perform the change
4. compare result to the preservation list
5. log any intentional semantic change

This is the creative equivalent of a round-trip integrity check. It catches elegant rewrites that quietly change motive, chronology, evidence, or canon.
