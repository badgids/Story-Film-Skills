# Creative Research Rules

Research exists to give the writer or filmmaker usable evidence, not to replace creative judgment.

## Start with a question

Write the exact factual or craft question before searching. Include what the story currently assumes so research can expose contradictions instead of merely collecting facts.

## Source preference

Use the strongest source available for the question:

- primary records and first-person material for historical detail
- official or standards documentation for technical behavior
- practitioner accounts for operational detail in medicine, law, trades, military work, craft, or other domains
- credible scholarship and reference works for interpretation and context
- interviews, criticism, reviews, and community discussion for reception or craft conventions

Published fiction may show how another writer handled a problem, but it is not evidence that a real-world fact is true.

## Report fields

A research note should contain:

- **Question**: what was investigated
- **Usable findings**: concrete details the project can employ
- **Source notes**: source type and why it is useful
- **Confidence**: high, medium, or low for claims where uncertainty matters
- **Conflicts**: credible sources that disagree
- **Contradictions**: findings that conflict with current story assumptions
- **Gaps**: material that could not be established
- **Story implications**: what changes if the project adopts the finding

## Fact versus inspiration

Label material as one of:

- verified fact
- supported interpretation
- contested claim
- anecdotal detail
- creative inspiration
- project decision

Do not turn a plausible anecdote or genre convention into canon as if research proved it.

## Current facts

When accuracy depends on current law, software, product capability, schedules, prices, public roles, medicine, safety guidance, or other changing facts, use current sources rather than model memory.

## Adoption

Research notes do not alter canon by themselves. Canon changes only when the project explicitly adopts the fact or creative decision.
