# Film Grammar Reference

Read `DRAMATURGY_RULES.md` first for scene and shot purpose. This file supplies production vocabulary and continuity rules.

## Shot purpose first

Before naming a lens or camera move, state what the shot must reveal, hide, compare, isolate, connect, pressure, or make the audience anticipate.

## Framing vocabulary

Use ordinary production terms:

- extreme wide
- wide
- full
- medium wide
- medium
- medium close-up
- close-up
- extreme close-up
- over-the-shoulder
- two-shot
- insert
- point of view

## Camera movement

Use movement only when it has a reason:

- locked-off
- pan
- tilt
- push in
- pull back
- track
- dolly
- crane
- handheld follow
- orbit
- rack focus

Describe start and end framing for meaningful moves. Record the movement reason separately when the shot schema supports it.

## Screen geography

Maintain eyelines, entrances, exits, dominant screen direction, and the 180-degree axis unless a deliberate break has a stated audience purpose.

A fast sequence still needs a readable map of subject, obstacle, goal, and escape direction.

### Match on action

When one action continues across a cut, preserve its phase and direction. If a hand begins closing on a handle in one angle, the next angle should continue that same reach rather than restart it unless the discontinuity is intentional.

### Eyeline match

When a character looks toward something, the answering shot should respect the established direction and approximate spatial relationship. A reaction and its object should read as one exchange of attention.

### Axis crossing

If the camera crosses the 180-degree axis, reorient the viewer with a neutral setup, visible move across the axis, or another deliberate geographic cue. Do not reverse left/right relationships accidentally.

## Eye trace

At a cut, know where the audience is looking. When possible, place the next important visual information where the eye can find it without a search.

## Coverage

Coverage is not a pile of random angles. Design enough material to cut the intended performance and rhythm. Favor fewer motivated setups over redundant coverage.

## Lens language

If exact focal length is not important, describe the optical effect instead of inventing a number. Examples: compressed background, neutral perspective, close wide-angle intimacy, shallow focus, deep focus.

## Lighting

State source logic and contrast before decorative adjectives. Example: soft north-window key, weak warm practical in background, negative fill camera-left.

Track practical sources and time-of-day changes when they affect continuity.

## Sound

Plan production sound, ambience, dialogue, music, effects, and deliberate silence as part of the shot. Do not postpone every sound decision to post.

## Transitions

A transition earns its place when picture, movement, sound, eye trace, idea, or dramatic state carries across the cut. Do not add transitions just to make the edit feel busy.
